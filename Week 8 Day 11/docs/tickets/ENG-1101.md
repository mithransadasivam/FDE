# ENG-1101 · Train the small model only on what the teacher is sure of

**Type:** feature · **Priority:** Must · **Day 11, Activity 1** · **Story:** a small model that learns the pilot's skill, not its mistakes

## Story

As Martin Hale, the COO, I want a small model the bank runs itself to read most proof-of-address documents, trained on the pilot's own reads, so that the remediation programme does not send 1.2 million customers' documents to Sonnet 5 one by one. As Helen Achebe, the MLRO, I want it to be at least as accurate as the pilot (ADR-003).

## The problem today

The pilot has kept 1,500 reads from November and December (`data/Day11_Slide10_teacher_reads.jsonl`): for every document, Sonnet's parser reading, which cites the words it used, and its vision reading. Nobody keyed these documents. In `backend/app/distil/labels.py`, `word_labels()` labels every word cited by the parser reading and keeps **every** read.

The teacher reads the statement period's end ("... to 31 Aug 2026") as the statement date on about one bank statement in five. Trained on every read, the small model learns that mistake: on **Model lab → Train**, the October batch's statement dates are right only **78%** of the time.

## Acceptance criteria

1. `word_labels(read)` gives one label per word, in the order of `read["words"]`: the field whose parser reading cites the word, or `"O"`.
2. It returns `None` (leave the read out) when, for any of the four fields, the parser value and the vision value disagree (`agree(read, field)`, which ignores case, spaces and punctuation), or the parser reading cites no words.
3. On the 1,500 pilot reads, **1,153** are kept and **347** left out.
4. Retrained on **Model lab → Train**, the small model gets **98.2%** of the October batch's fields right on its own (statement dates 93.0%). Every score compares with Tom's double-keyed labels, never with the teacher.
