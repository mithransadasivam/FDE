# ENG-1002 · Chase the applicant on a clock that survives a crash

**Type:** feature · **Priority:** Must · **Day 10, Activity 2** · **Story:** the reminder goes on day 3, whatever happens to the worker

## Story

As Grace Mensah, team lead for North, I want every applicant we ask for documents to get a reminder after 3 days and the case escalated to me after 7, exactly as the playbook says (data/Day10_Slide10_chase_policy.json), so that no case waits for weeks because nobody remembered to chase.

## The problem today

`chase()` in `backend/app/workflow/chase.py` keeps its own clock: it notes `time.time()` when the request goes, then sleeps half a day at a time and asks the document store again. That holds a thread for days, and it calls the bank every half day. Worse: after a crash DBOS runs the workflow again from the top, `time.time()` gives a new start, and the clock begins again. Crash the worker on day 2 of KX-1004's wait, and the reminder goes on day 5, not day 3.

## Acceptance criteria

1. `chase(key, ticket_id, policy)` follows its docstring: the request, then `DBOS.recv(DOCUMENTS, timeout_seconds=engine.days(policy["reminder_after_days"]))`, the reminder, the rest of the wait (`escalate_after_days - reminder_after_days`), then `steps.escalate()`.
2. As soon as a document arrives (the console sends the webhook's event to the workflow on the topic `DOCUMENTS`), call `steps.received()` and return it. Return `None` once the case is escalated.
3. The days come from `policy`, never written into the code.
4. `chase()` never reads the clock and never sleeps: DBOS saves each wait's deadline, so a crash cannot move it (CLAUDE.md rule 18).
5. On **Workflows**: start KX-1004, approve it, and crash the worker on day 2 of its wait. The reminder goes 3 days after the request, the escalation 7 days after.
